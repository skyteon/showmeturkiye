COP31 Antalya 2026 the UN climate talks

Dates, venue, access and official links for the UN Climate Change Conference, 9–20 November 2026, and the landscapes the talks are about.

Show Me Türkiye October 2026 7 min read
Dates9–20 November 2026Two weeks of negotiations
VenueAntalya EXPO CenterAksu district, east of the city
PresidencyTürkiye hostsAustralia leads the negotiations
Official sitesunfccc.int/cop31cop31.tr for the host country

COP31, the United Nations Climate Change Conference, takes place in Antalya, Türkiye, from 9 to 20 November 2026, at the Antalya EXPO Center in the Aksu district east of the city. For two weeks, government delegations from almost every country will negotiate the next steps on climate action, while observers, businesses, researchers, journalists and members of the public gather around the talks.

This guide explains what COP31 is, why it matters, who can attend and how to plan a visit to Antalya. It is an independent guide: for registration and official information, use the conference websites listed below.

02The basics

What is COP31?

COP stands for Conference of the Parties. The Parties are the 198 members of the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC), the treaty agreed in 1992 to stabilise greenhouse gas concentrations in the atmosphere: 197 countries plus the European Union. They have met every year since 1995, apart from 2020, to negotiate how that goal is put into practice. COP31 is the thirty-first of those meetings. The same fortnight also hosts the twenty-first session of the Kyoto Protocol meeting (CMP 21) and the eighth session of the Paris Agreement meeting (CMA 8), which is why the official programme lists all three.

198Parties to the convention
1995First COP, Berlin. Annual since, apart from 2020
1.5°CThe Paris Agreement's ambition for limiting warming

The best-known result of the process is the Paris Agreement of 2015, under which countries aim to hold warming well below 2°C and pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5°C. Each country sets its own targets, called nationally determined contributions, and is expected to strengthen them over time. The annual COPs are where governments review progress, agree rules and negotiate the decisions that hold the system together.

A COP is two events at once. In the negotiating rooms, government delegations work through a formal agenda, alongside meetings of the bodies that serve the Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement. Around them, thousands of observers, businesses, researchers, journalists and local visitors take part in exhibitions, side events and public programmes.

03Why it matters

Why COP31 matters.

More than a decade after Paris, the focus of the talks has moved from writing rules to delivering on them. The host country's stated aim for COP31 is to move "from negotiation to implementation". In practice, the conference returns to several long-running questions:

  1. iCutting emissions Whether national targets add up to the Paris goals, and how quickly they are being met.
  2. iiAdaptation How countries prepare for heat, drought, floods and rising seas that are already happening.
  3. iiiClimate finance How money reaches developing countries. At COP29 in Baku, countries agreed a new goal under which developed countries take the lead in mobilising at least 300 billion US dollars a year for developing countries by 2035.
  4. ivNature and ecosystems Forests, oceans and biodiversity, which both store carbon and suffer from warming.
  5. vAccountability How countries report what they do, so that pledges can be checked.

Alongside the formal negotiations, the host runs an Action Agenda. Its ten published themes are zero waste, oceans and seas, food security, climate-resilient cities, the Climate Implementation Bridge, youth and education, green industrialisation, the clean energy transition, Rio synergy, and dynamic and resilient health systems. These are the host's priorities for events and initiatives, not items on the negotiating agenda. The decisions themselves are negotiated and agreed by the Parties during the conference; none of them is settled in advance.

04The host

Why Antalya and Türkiye?

The decision on who would host COP31 was taken at COP30 in Belém, Brazil, in November 2025. Türkiye and Australia had both bid, and the outcome was a shared arrangement: Türkiye hosts the conference in Antalya and holds the COP31 Presidency, while Australia's climate minister leads the negotiations as President of Negotiations.

Antalya is Türkiye's busiest holiday region, so it already has what a conference of this size needs: an international airport with routes across Europe and beyond, a large exhibition centre, and tens of thousands of hotel rooms. The organisers describe it as the city of blue and green, where the Mediterranean meets forested mountains. The same coast and forests that draw visitors are also exposed to heat, drought and wildfire.

For our full picture of the city and its surroundings, see the Antalya travel guide and the Antalya city guide.

05Context

Climate change and Türkiye.

Four landscapes from our archive, and what is changing around them.

Türkiye lies in the Mediterranean basin, which the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) describes as a climate change hotspot. Regional assessments by the Mediterranean Experts on Climate and Environmental Change (MedECC) find that the basin is warming faster than the global average, and it is projected to become drier. The effects differ across a country this large.

Forests and wildfire

Forests that live with fire.

The pine forests of the south and west coasts are adapted to fire, but longer, hotter and drier summers raise the risk. In the summer of 2021, large wildfires burned in several coastal provinces, including the Manavgat area of Antalya.

Water

Rain, snowmelt and the lakes.

Much of the country depends on rain and snowmelt that vary a great deal from year to year. Lower rainfall and higher evaporation put pressure on reservoirs, farming and lakes, and water resources are a central theme in Türkiye's climate adaptation planning.

Seasons and coasts

A longer summer, a higher sea.

Hotter summers, milder winters and heavier downpours change when and how people farm, travel and live. Low-lying coastal areas and deltas are exposed to rising seas and storms.

Mountains

Snow that feeds the rivers.

High ranges such as the Kaçkar in the north-east hold snowfields and small glaciers that feed rivers through the summer. Warming shortens the snow season and can push plants and animals to higher ground.

06Attending

How to attend COP31.

The most important thing to understand is that COP31 has two separate areas with separate rules. Not everyone can enter the conference venue.

Accredited participants only

The Blue Zone

UN-managed. Where the negotiations take place.

Entry requires accreditation through the UNFCCC. In practice, that means one of three routes:

  • Party delegates: nominated by their national government through its official focal point.
  • Observers: representatives of organisations already admitted as UNFCCC observers, nominated by their organisation's designated contact point.
  • Media: journalists apply for press accreditation, which is administered by the UNFCCC secretariat.

Individuals cannot buy a ticket or register themselves for the Blue Zone. The UNFCCC states that registration and badges are free of charge for duly nominated participants. Nominations and confirmations go through the UNFCCC Online Registration System; for COP31 it opened in July 2026, and the confirmation window for Parties and admitted observer organisations closes on 8 November 2026.

Open to the public

The Green Zone

Coordinated by the host country, for the wider public.

According to the official COP31 FAQ, it is open to the public, students, NGOs, companies and researchers.

  • Entry: generally free, and pre-registration is required in most cases through the official COP31 website or the Green Zone registration platform. On-site registration is possible at some events with limited capacity.
  • Bring: a valid ID (passport, driving licence or national ID card). Bag searches, X-ray scanning and metal detector checks may be applied.
  • Programme: panels, workshops, exhibitions, film screenings, concerts, cultural talks and networking events, mostly in Turkish, with some English sessions. Hours are generally 09:00 to 20:00, varying by event.

Organisations that want to host an event or exhibit in the Green Zone can apply through the COP31 website.

Details, opening hours and registration can change, so treat the official pages as the final word.

07Practical

Practical Antalya visitor information.

Getting there

Antalya Airport (AYT) is the main gateway, with international and domestic flights. The venue, the Antalya EXPO Center, is in the Aksu district east of the city. The official FAQ says the venue is reached from the airport by road and that dedicated COP31 shuttle services will be arranged; the official site publishes transport and digital map information.

Getting around during the conference

According to the official FAQ, the Blue Zone is served by public transport, shuttle services, taxis and private transport, and transport is free for accredited participants. The Green Zone is reached by public transport, tram, shuttle services and walking paths; the FAQ does not recommend private vehicles. Green Zone hours are given as generally 09:00 to 20:00, varying by event. The UNFCCC's participant information adds that the shuttles are complimentary and stop only at hotels listed on the official COP31 accommodation platform, so participants staying elsewhere may need to reach the nearest stop on their own.

Where to stay

The official FAQ directs bookings to the host's official accommodation partner, cop31.coraltravel.com, and says accommodation guides will be published on the COP31 portal. Hotels booked through that platform are also the ones served by the official shuttle network. If you are not part of an official delegation, check how far your hotel is from the venue and from public transport.

Visas

The official FAQ says a visa is required for most international participants and that special visa processes may apply for COP participants. The official site has a dedicated visa and entry page; follow the process described there.

November weather

Antalya stays mild in November: the UNFCCC's participant information gives typical daytime temperatures of 18°C to 22°C, with cooler evenings, and it is also when the winter rains begin. Pack layers, a light waterproof and comfortable shoes for long days on your feet.

A free day

Kaleiçi, the Düden waterfalls, Perge and Aspendos are close to the city, and the coast around Kemer is about an hour away. Our Antalya guide and Kemer guide cover the days around the conference.

09FAQ

Questions we get asked.

Short answers to the most common ones.

When is COP31?

COP31 runs from 9 to 20 November 2026.

Where will COP31 take place?

In Antalya, on the Mediterranean coast of Türkiye. The official COP31 FAQ gives the venue as the Antalya EXPO Center in the Aksu district.

What does COP stand for?

Conference of the Parties: the annual meeting of the Parties to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change. COP31 is the thirty-first.

Is COP31 open to the public?

Partly. The Blue Zone, where negotiations happen, is only for accredited participants. The host-run Green Zone is open to the public, generally free of charge, and usually requires pre-registration.

How do I register?

For the Blue Zone, you need to be nominated by a national government, by an admitted observer organisation, or accredited as media through the UNFCCC. For the Green Zone, register through the official COP31 website, cop31.tr.

Where is the official COP31 website?

The UN conference page is unfccc.int/cop31 and the host country site is cop31.tr. This article is an independent guide and is not an official source.

Why is COP31 important?

It is where governments review whether climate commitments are being met and negotiate the next steps on cutting emissions, adaptation and climate finance, more than a decade after the Paris Agreement.

10Closing

Antalya, and why it matters.

Most people will know COP31 through headlines about negotiations and final decisions. For anyone who comes to Antalya, it is also a chance to see the landscapes the talks are about: a warm sea, pine forests that live with fire, rivers fed by mountain snow, and a coast that millions of people depend on.

We will keep this guide up to date as official details are confirmed. Until then, the safest rule is simple: check the official websites before you book, and check them again before you travel.
